1The short answer

We reproduce CamCASP's machinery essentially exactly and its numbers only partly. Every stage of the route is the same stage, and three of the six stages agree to within their own numerical noise. The published discrepancy has exactly four sources, and they enter at two places in the chain.

① The response kernel is ~3% soft

Our site-summed rank-1 polarizability is 0.971 of CamCASP's, and the two independent CamCASP oracles agree with each other on that total to 0.11% — so the gap is upstream of the partition. The leading suspect is documented: CamCASP density-fits the two-electron integrals that enter its own CKS Hessian in a 246-function Cartesian auxiliary basis, while we build \(G(i\omega)\) from exact integrals. The two routes therefore do not “share everything except how the FDDS is distributed”.

② The row-weight convention is a real solver error

Hand our WSM solver CamCASP's own localized anchor and its own 500-point response — a hermetic replay with no upstream error at all — and with the production default it still returns \(a_3\) at 0.6955 of target, with a maximum parameter deviation of 191.6. Set the relative column-norm cutoff to zero and the same solver returns 1.0192; switch the row weights to unique-pair-equal as well and it returns 1.0000 to 7.6e‑5. Only the second half of that survives the move to our own inputs: on our own design matrix the cutoff prunes nothing, while unique-pair-equal improves every published quantity.

③ Our own fit grid destroys rank 2

The replay does not reproduce the rank-2 collapse: on CamCASP's inputs \(a_2\) comes back at 0.9701, but the full pipeline publishes 0.6810. That residue is an interaction between our 329-point grid and our own point response — neither input alone reproduces it. Our innermost shell sits at 4.5 bohr offset, inside the region where an L3 model provably cannot represent the response, so model error projects coherently onto the rank-2 columns.

④ Everything downstream is exact

Replay CamCASP's own refined response through our Casimir–Polder integrator and we reproduce CamCASP's printed molecular C6…C12 totals to 1–2 parts in 108. The frequency grid is bit-identical. The dispersion errors you see in section 11 are entirely inherited from the polarizabilities; not one part of them is made in the integrator.

The one-line version. Ranks 1, 2 and 3 track CamCASP to within 3% all the way through the non-local response and the localization. The 30% deficit appears inside the WSM refinement, and it is not one bug but two: a solver-policy bug that we can reproduce and switch off, and a fit-grid mismatch that we can characterise but not yet fix.

The best configuration found so far stacks three switchable changes and beats the shipped default by 5–6× on every dispersion coefficient (§8.7): the basis-space ISA partition, the innermost fit shell moved from 4.5 to 6.5 bohr, and the published ISA-Pol anchor weight gated to rank 1. It also improves the static and dynamic polarizabilities by 5.18×, which is more than it improves C8, and which takes α from outside its parity band to inside it (§8.8) — so it is not buying the Casimir–Polder integral at the integrand’s expense. The radius is the largest of the three levers, and the anchor is the only one measured here that is orthogonal to another: it applies the same absolute correction inside either partition, to a tenth of a percent. That orthogonality is a statement about the correction, though, not about the score — on α the anchor is worth nothing once the partition is in place, because the binding error moves to a component it cannot reach. Every one of these is implemented and switchable, and every one still defaults to the reviewed behaviour, because one of the three is still an empirical scan rather than a derivation.

2The two routes, side by side

Both codes compute the same object by the same sequence of operations. The table below is the route, stage for stage, with the verdict in the gutter. Note which differences are choices (our second partition arm; our grid) and which are artifacts of what CamCASP is (its density-fitted Hessian; its exchange-symmetry print convention).

What is genuinely shared. The Kohn–Sham reference (PBE0 with a GRAC asymptotic correction in aug-cc-pVTZ), the Lillestolen–Wheatley redistribution rule, the eleven-point frequency grid (a static point plus ten Gauss–Legendre half-line nodes, reproduced to 10−10), the L3 model space (15 components, ranks 1–3), and the Casimir–Polder quadrature. Where we have been able to feed our machinery CamCASP's own intermediates, it returns CamCASP's own answers.

One difference is not ours to close. The reference protocol's own specification claims the ISA and density-fitting routes “share everything except how the FDDS is distributed over sites.” That is not strictly true: CamCASP density-fits the response matrix itself, so the two-electron integrals inside its CKS Hessians are already approximated in the 246-function Cartesian auxiliary basis. We compute \(G(i\omega)\) from exact integrals. Matching CamCASP exactly at rank 1 would mean deliberately degrading our own kernel.

3The object we are both computing

The target is the frequency-dependent density susceptibility (FDDS) \(\chi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';i\omega)\), reduced to a set of site–site polarizability tensors. The molecular polarizability is the double contraction of \(\chi\) with multipole operators,

$$ \alpha_{tu}(i\omega) \;=\; -\!\int\!\!\!\int\! d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\; \hat Q_t(\mathbf r)\;\chi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';i\omega)\;\hat Q_u(\mathbf r'), $$

and the distributed model puts one atom-centred weight function on each side, giving an ordered site-pair tensor:

$$ \alpha^{ab}_{tu}(i\omega) \;=\; -\!\int\!\!\!\int\! d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\; w_a(\mathbf r)\,\hat Q^a_t(\mathbf r)\;\chi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';i\omega)\; w_b(\mathbf r')\,\hat Q^b_u(\mathbf r'), \qquad \sum_{ab}\alpha^{ab}_{tu}=\alpha_{tu}. $$

Everything in this document is a disagreement about one of three things: what \(\chi\) is (section 5), what \(w_a\) is (section 6), or how the \(3\times3\) array of site-pair tensors is collapsed onto three single-site tensors and then refined (sections 7–8).

The scalar we track throughout is the rank invariant, the isotropic part of each diagonal rank block — frame-independent, so it cannot be moved by a rotation convention:

$$ a_\ell \;=\; \frac{1}{2\ell+1}\operatorname{Tr}\,\alpha^{(\ell\ell)}, \qquad \ell = 1,2,3 . $$

In the L3 model the 15 components are ordered 10, 11c, 11s | 20, 21c, 21s, 22c, 22s | 30, 31c, 31s, 32c, 32s, 33c, 33s, so rank \(\ell\) occupies the block starting at \(\ell^2-1\) with dimension \(2\ell+1\). A site tensor has \(15\cdot16/2 = 120\) upper-triangle variables; three sites give 360, of which the PDef symmetry mask leaves 170 active (O 38, H1 66, H2 66) and 66 equality rows reduce to 104 independent parameters. Both codes fit the same 104.

5Stage A — the response kernel

~2.9% deficit Both codes solve the coupled Kohn–Sham equations at imaginary frequency for the density–density response. In an auxiliary product basis \(\{\phi_P\}\) this is the usual Dyson-like resummation,

$$ G(i\omega) \;=\; \bigl[\,\Lambda^{-1}(i\omega) \;-\; K\,\bigr]^{-1}, \qquad \Lambda_{PQ}(i\omega) \;=\; \sum_{ia} \frac{4\,\varepsilon_{ai}\,(P|ia)(ia|Q)}{\varepsilon_{ai}^{2}+\omega^{2}} , $$

with \(K\) the Coulomb-plus-exchange-correlation kernel and \(\varepsilon_{ai}=\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_i\). The reference determinant is identical on both sides (PBE0 with a GRAC asymptotic correction, aug-cc-pVTZ), and the eleven-point frequency grid is bit-identical. What is not identical is how the integrals inside \(\Lambda\) are obtained.

What remains is the underlying CKS response itself — the functional, the GRAC asymptotic correction and the exchange-correlation kernel. The subsection below reaches the last piece of that which the two recipes actually disagree on, and closes it off too.

The last stage-A degree of freedom: ALDA correlation

Stripped to their definitions the two response kernels are the same object. Both are 25% coupled Hartree–Fock plus 75% ALDA; both take XC_LDA_X as the ALDA exchange; both correct the reference potential asymptotically, and this code builds its GRAC shift self-consistently from the computed ionisation potential exactly as the reviewed recipe prescribes. Auditing the two term by term leaves precisely one difference: CamCASP builds the ALDA half from the PW91 correlation functional, whose local (LSDA) limit is PW92, while this code has always used VWN.

That difference is now a keyword. ATOMIC_POLARIZABILITY_ALDA_CORRELATION selects VWN (XC_LDA_C_VWN, the shipped default, which every committed reference was produced with) or PW91 (XC_LDA_C_PW, the PW92 local limit — the reviewed convention). The arm is checked against the published parameterisation rather than against a second LibXC call: a hand-written evaluation of Perdew & Wang's eqn (10) with the Table I paramagnetic parameters, twice-differentiated numerically, reproduces the kernel the arm actually builds to 1.6×10−7 relative. The two correlation functionals differ from each other by 0.3–1.5% on \(f_{xc}\) over the density range sampled, a thousandfold above that verification floor, so the arm demonstrably moves the kernel it is supposed to move.

6Stage B — the partition fork

ISA arm matches C-DF arm is a different model This is where “which electrons belong to which atom” is decided, and it is the largest source of ambiguity in the whole comparison — far larger than any disagreement between us and CamCASP. We implement both arms CamCASP offers.

Arm 1 — iterated stockholder atoms (real space)

ISA assigns real-space weights self-consistently from spherically averaged atomic shape functions:

$$ w_a(\mathbf r)=\frac{\sigma_a(|\mathbf r-\mathbf R_a|)} {\sum_b \sigma_b(|\mathbf r-\mathbf R_b|)}, \qquad \sigma_a(s)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\!\oint\! d\Omega\; w_a(\mathbf R_a+\mathbf s)\,\rho(\mathbf R_a+\mathbf s), $$

iterated to self-consistency. We reproduce this arm closely, including one quirk of CamCASP's bounded-variable least squares that we replicate deliberately: only s-shells with exponents in [0, 0.5] are given a lower bound.

Arm 2 — constrained density fitting

The C-DF arm fits the density in an atom-labelled auxiliary basis and assigns whole auxiliary functions to sites. Here the specification and the implementation genuinely describe different models. The spec writes a hard constraint,

$$ \min_{\mathbf d}\;\Delta[\mathbf d] \quad\text{subject to}\quad C\mathbf d=\mathbf n, \qquad \Delta[\mathbf d]=\bigl\langle \rho-\tilde\rho \,\big|\, \rho-\tilde\rho \bigr\rangle , $$

whereas the oracle we compare against solves a penalised problem with a finite weight, plus an inter-site localisation term:

$$ \min_{\mathbf d}\;\Delta[\mathbf d] \;+\; \lambda\,\bigl\|C\mathbf d-\mathbf n\bigr\|^{2} \;-\; \eta \sum_{a}\sum_{b\neq a} E^{ab}[\mathbf d], \qquad \lambda=1.0,\;\; \eta=5\times10^{-4}. $$

The hard constraint is the \(\lambda\to\infty\) limit, and it gives a different partition. With the production \(\lambda=1\) the charge constraint is satisfied only to 4.57×10−5, and the localization step amplifies that residual by a factor of 14.7 into a 6.73×10−4 charge-sum residual downstream. The auxiliary metric is also badly conditioned: 1.902×1012 bare, rising to 7.798×1012 once the penalties are added. Anyone reading the spec's equations and expecting our DF numbers is comparing against a model that neither code solves.

7Stage C — LW localization

same rule, 2% residue The non-local array has nine ordered site pairs; the model has three single-site tensors. Lillestolen–Wheatley redistributes the off-diagonal pairs onto the diagonal along the bond graph, preserving the molecular total exactly. The charge-flow sum rule it enforces, and the conservation law it respects, are

$$ \sum_b \alpha^{ab}_{t,00}\;=\;0 \quad \forall\,a,t \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum_{a}\tilde\alpha^{aa}_{tu}\;=\;\sum_{ab}\alpha^{ab}_{tu}. $$

No polarizability is created or destroyed — only moved. Both codes use the same rule and the same bond graph. 8Stage D — WSM refinement the break

The localized model reproduces the molecular total but not the response to a probe charge sitting outside the molecule. Refinement fixes that: sample the exact response at a cloud of external points, and adjust the 104 independent model parameters to reproduce it, while a penalty keeps the low-rank blocks near their localized values. Written out, both codes solve

$$ \min_{\mathbf x}\; \bigl\|\,W\,(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)\,\bigr\|^{2} \;+\;\lambda\,\bigl\|\,D\,(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)\,\bigr\|^{2} \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad C\mathbf x=\mathbf d , $$

where \(A\mathbf x\) is the model's predicted point response, \(\mathbf b\) the exact point response, \(W\) the row weights, \(\mathbf x_0\) the localized anchor and \(C\mathbf x=\mathbf d\) the 66 PDef equality (COPY) rows. We form no normal equations: the equality elimination and the reduced fit are both direct SVDs. The penalty term is Stone's eqn (9.3.13), which in its general form couples parameters,

$$ Z \;=\; \sum_k w_k \bigl|\Delta V_k\bigr|^{2} \;+\; \sum_{p\,p'} g_{pp'}\, \bigl(x_p-x_p^{0}\bigr)\bigl(x_{p'}-x_{p'}^{0}\bigr), \qquad g_{pp'} \;\longrightarrow\; \lambda\,D_p^{2}\,\delta_{pp'} \;\text{ here.} $$

and which Stone introduces (§9.3.4) precisely to stabilise buried atoms whose high-rank blocks the external points cannot resolve. Our implementation anchors a block when both of its component ranks are at or below anchor_rank_limit, default 1.

Finding ② — the production solver policy is wrong on CamCASP's inputs, and it is reproducible. The cutoff is relative: columns are dropped when their weighted norm falls below \(10^{-4}\times\max_j\|W A_{:j}\|\). On this design matrix that prunes three columns, and those three columns carry real rank-3 information. Turning the cutoff off recovers \(a_3\) to 1.0192, and additionally switching the row weights from full-symmetric-Frobenius (which applies a \(\sqrt2\) to off-diagonal pairs) to unique-pair-equal recovers all three invariants to 7.6×10−5 in the parameters themselves. The maximum parameter deviation under the production policy is 191.6 — this is not a rounding effect.

Only half of finding ② transfers to our own pipeline. Both policies are now switchable Psi4 keywords (ATOMIC_POLARIZABILITY_WSM_COLUMN_PRUNING and ATOMIC_POLARIZABILITY_WSM_ROW_WEIGHTS), so the replay result can be tested end to end rather than inferred. The cutoff arm is inert: the solver publishes its own diagnostics, and they record 0 of 170 columns pruned at every one of the 11 frequencies, with the absolute cutoff moving 7.378×10−6 → 0 to prove the keyword reached the kernel. Pruning three columns is a property of CamCASP's design matrix, not of the policy. The row-weight arm does transfer, and improves all five published quantities — worst static component 0.17559 → 0.16887 and C8 0.25155 → 0.22315. The two levers factorize exactly: the full 2×2 is two distinct rows, not four.

Finding ③ — rank 2 is not explained by the solver. The same replay returns \(a_2\) at 0.9701, but the full pipeline publishes 0.6810. Feeding CamCASP's inputs therefore fixes most of rank 2, which means the rank-2 collapse is a property of our own inputs: our 329-point fit grid crossed with our own point-response evaluation. Note that even at 0.9701 the per-site distribution is already wrecked under the production cutoff — oxygen \(a_2\) at 0.4757 against hydrogen \(a_2\) at 2.988 — so the sum surviving is a cancellation, not health.

8.2 The fit grid

The point cloud is the second half of the story. The reviewed protocol's point-to-point grid spans 4.63 to 11.46 bohr from the nearest nucleus. Ours is a 329-point nested_equidistant_lebedev_surfaces selection (750 candidates, cap 500) on five radial shells at offsets 4.5, 6.25, 8.0, 9.75 and 11.5 bohr, Lebedev order 11, 50 spherical points per shell.

An L3 model cannot represent the true response at short range — the multipole expansion is not convergent there. Sampling inside that region does not merely add noise; it adds a coherent model error that the least-squares fit will happily absorb by distorting whichever columns are cheapest, and under a rank-truncating SVD the cheapest columns are exactly the weakly-determined rank-2 and rank-3 ones. That is our working hypothesis for the residual rank-2 deficit; it is consistent with everything measured but has not yet been isolated by a grid-only experiment.

8.4 What we found instead: gate the published anchor weight

§8.3 refutes the rank axis of the penalty. It says nothing about the weight. Our reviewed policy puts unit weight on every anchored block, but eqn (22) of the ISA-Pol paper does not — it scales each parameter's penalty by its own localized value,

$$ g_{kk'} \;=\; \delta_{kk'}\,\frac{w_0}{1+\bigl(p_k^{0}\bigr)^{2}} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad D_k \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\bigl(p_k^{0}\bigr)^{2}}},\quad \lambda = w_0 . $$

Dropped in as published this changes two things at once: it rescales the weight, and — because the published sum runs over every fitted parameter — it removes the rank gate. Measured against the dispersion oracle those two have opposite signs, so a third keyword setting ISA-POL-GATED applies the rescaling behind the gate and lets each be attributed on its own.

9Stage E — Casimir–Polder

exact to 1e-7 The dispersion coefficients follow from a frequency integral over the imaginary axis. For the isotropic leading term,

$$ C_6^{ab} \;=\; \frac{3}{\pi}\int_0^{\infty}\!\!d\omega\; \bar\alpha^{a}(i\omega)\,\bar\alpha^{b}(i\omega), \qquad \bar\alpha^{a}(i\omega)=\tfrac13\operatorname{Tr}\,\alpha^{a(11)}(i\omega), $$

and generally \(C_n\) couples ranks with \(n=2(\ell_a+\ell_b+1)\). The half-line is mapped to \([-1,1]\) and integrated with ten Gauss–Legendre nodes plus one zero-weight static point:

$$ \omega(t) \;=\; s\,\frac{1+t}{1-t}, \qquad \int_0^\infty f(\omega)\,d\omega \;=\; \int_{-1}^{1} f\bigl(\omega(t)\bigr)\,\frac{2s}{(1-t)^2}\,dt . $$

The resulting eleven-point grid is identical on both sides to 10−10; the implementation refuses to run if it does not find exactly ten non-zero-weight nodes. Replaying CamCASP's own refined response through our integrator gives its own printed molecular totals back:

10Stage F — anisotropic recoupling

convention, not accuracy The anisotropic dispersion expansion labels each coefficient by the two local ranks and a coupling index:

$$ E_{\rm disp} \;=\; -\sum_{n}\;\sum_{\ell_1 k_1,\,\ell_2 k_2,\,j} \frac{C_n^{\ell_1k_1,\,\ell_2k_2,\,j}}{R^{\,n}}\; S^{\ell_1k_1,\,\ell_2k_2,\,j}\bigl(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega\bigr), $$

subject to the triangle and parity selection rules

$$ |\ell_1-\ell_2|\;\le\; j \;\le\; \ell_1+\ell_2, \qquad n \equiv j \pmod 2, \qquad n \;\ge\; m(\ell_1)+m(\ell_2)+2 . $$

Keying the parity rule on \(j\) rather than on \(\ell_1+\ell_2\) matters: the \(j\) form produces zero violations against the oracle's label set, while the \(\ell_1+\ell_2\) form wrongly rejects 2968 labels that the oracle prints.

Our harmonics are Racah-normalised, \(C_{\ell m}(\hat r)=\sqrt{4\pi/(2\ell+1)}\;Y_{\ell m}(\hat r)\), and the interaction tensor carries a bare binomial:

$$ T^{c}_{\ell_a m_a,\,\ell_b m_b} \;=\; (-1)^{\ell_b}\sqrt{\binom{2L}{2\ell_a}}\; \langle \ell_a m_a;\ell_b m_b \,|\, L M\rangle\; \frac{C^{*}_{LM}(\hat{\mathbf R})}{R^{\,L+1}}, \qquad L=\ell_a+\ell_b . $$

Two open convention questions

i Exchange symmetry

Measured over all 979 O–O and 3466 H–H labels, the oracle satisfies

\(C_n^{\ell_2k_2,\ell_1k_1,j}=C_n^{\ell_1k_1,\ell_2k_2,j}\)

i.e. plain symmetry under site exchange, whereas we produce \((-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2}\). The two agree wherever \(\ell_1+\ell_2\) is even and differ by a sign on 424 of the 979 O–O labels — a relative deviation of exactly 2, which is the signature of a pure sign flip rather than a magnitude error.

ii The j-dependent factor

An exact Clebsch–Gordan factor

\(1\big/\bigl|\langle \ell_1 0;\ell_2 0|j\,0\rangle\bigr|\)

separates our coefficients from the oracle's. It is exact, not approximate, which means it is a normalisation convention in the \(S\)-functions and not an error in the response. No internal consistency check can decide which side owns it — that requires either the S-function normalisation and phase from the primary source, or an independent third implementation.

Both of these are labelling disagreements: they change which number is printed under which label and with which sign, not how much dispersion energy the model contains. They are recorded in-source as explicitly unverified, alongside the residual real sign per \((\ell_1,\ell_2,j)\) and a possible \((2j+1)^{1/2}\). None of them contributes to the isotropic errors in section 11.

13.4 The cliff was the column cutoff, not the grid

The failure at the end of 13.2 is specific and mechanical, and reading the solver explains it without any new measurement. refine_wsm prunes design columns whose weighted norm falls below a relative threshold, and then builds the equality-constraint matrix on the columns that survive. Those equality rows are pure symmetry copies: each carries +1 on one H2 variable and −χ on the matching H1 variable. Rows like that are automatically independent of one another, because each owns a distinct +1 column — so the only way the constraint matrix can lose rank is if pruning deletes the columns out from under it. A row whose two columns are both pruned becomes identically zero, which is exactly the constraint_rank < constraint_count throw.

That predicts the whole cliff from one keyword. As the innermost shell retreats, the columns only the near-field points constrain lose weighted norm fastest — high-rank columns most of all, since a rank-3 field falls off far quicker than a rank-1 one. 13.5 Axis frames: what we publish and what the reference speaks

Nothing else on this page depends on the frame, which is precisely why this section is easy to omit and worth stating. Every diagnostic driving 13.2 through 13.4 — the rank invariants aℓ = Tr(αℓℓ)/(2ℓ+1) and the isotropic Cn — is a rotational invariant. A frame error would not perturb a single number in any of those tables.

We are molecular-frame everywhere, by contract. Each site's local-to-global rotation is the identity, because the WSM design matrix is built from molecular-frame solid harmonics. This is enforced rather than assumed: the PDef mask must be derived with empty site axes, since the mask indexes variables in whichever frame it is handed and a mismatch against the design matrix would yield plausible-looking but wrong anisotropy. The general machinery exists — rank-1–3 Wigner rotations and the two-frame S functions are implemented and tested — but it is a verification seam, not the publication path.

The reference is not. A .pol block reports each site in its own local axes, and extraction rotates it into the molecular frame before anything is compared. For water this is benign: H1's local axes are the molecular axes turned 180° about z, so the correction is a sign flip on the x-odd components and costs no precision at all. Two guards watch it. The exact C2 relation αH2 = SxαH1Sx is asserted at zero tolerance and fails if either hydrogen is left in its own frame; and a separate test pins the PDef-mask hazard by showing that rotated local axes produce a different mask.

The C2 guard constrains the relation between the hydrogens, not the absolute convention. Any axis rule that respects the molecular C2v symmetry satisfies it, correct or not. What actually pins the absolute frame is the component-level static α comparison, and at 3.4% worst deviation in the best configuration it pins it tightly — a bond-aligned rotation on the hydrogens would show up far larger than that. The frame we use is right for the reference we hold; the point is that only one comparison is testing it.

The ingestion path is structurally narrow. Reference extraction accepts exactly one axis-rule form — local z is global Z, with x taken from a named bond — and the rotation helper refuses any frame that moves z, because then the real solid harmonics mix across |k| and a single angle can no longer describe the transformation. A Tinker-style automatic local-axis convention puts local z along the O–H bond, which is not a rotation about global Z. Such a file would be rejected at both guards rather than silently mis-rotated, which is the right failure mode, but it also means we cannot currently read one: the extractor would need full Wigner D matrices for ranks 1 through 3, which only the C++ side has.

The unvoiced exposure is the anisotropic set. The anisotropic Cn are frame-dependent by construction — the recoupling coefficients are a property of the two local frames, which is the whole point of the S-function split — and the production routine that publishes them takes no frame argument at all. Our anisotropic table is therefore implicitly “local frame = molecular frame” at every site, and nothing in the published arrays says so. Anyone comparing it against a reference table in per-site local axes is comparing across conventions. This is unlikely to be the explanation for F1 and F2: those are an exact (−1)ℓ1+ℓ2 sign and an exact CG factor on every label, whereas a frame rotation is messy and label-dependent. It is a separate assumption in the same code path, not the answer to those two. It does matter downstream: a force-field consumer needs local-frame coefficients, and no converter exists on the output side.

Acc. 137, 153 (2018) — the ISA-Pol paper — replaces the old WSM penalty, which it describes as “weak for the dipole-dipole polarizabilities, and completely absent for the higher ranking terms”, with eqn (22):

$$ g_{kk'} \;=\; \delta_{kk'}\,\frac{w_0}{1 + (p^0_k)^2} $$

Under this code's objective \(\min \lVert W(Ax-b)\rVert^2 + \lambda \lVert D(x-x^0)\rVert^2\) that is \(D_k = 1/\sqrt{1 + (p^0_k)^2}\) with \(\lambda = w_0\), applied at every rank rather than only at or below the anchored rank.
