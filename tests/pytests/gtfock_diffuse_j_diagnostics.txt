GTFock's J deficit on a diffuse basis: the probes behind the "Diffuse functions
on a large system are not supported" bullet
=============================================================================

doc/sphinxman/source/gtfock.rst states, under `Prototype scope`, that GTFock
cannot converge protein157 in Cartesian 6-31+G** (1863 basis functions) while
Psi4's own DirectJK and density fitting both converge the identical input. That
is the claim this file backs, and it is measured directly from the runs.

This file also once claimed to locate the fault -- in J rather than K, and in
the diffuse shells rather than the system size. It does not any more. Sections
5 and 6 are controls that were run late: section 5 overturned that attribution,
and section 6 localizes the failure to the very first SCF iteration and shows
that Psi4's own DirectJK takes that first step into the same near-null subspace
of the overlap, only far less deeply. The cause is still open. Sections 1
through 4 are preserved as run, because their measurements are sound even where
the conclusions drawn from them were not, and because the way they went wrong
is worth keeping.

Two kinds of evidence live in two different files, and the split is deliberate:

  * gtfock_hpc_multinode_provenance.txt holds the SCHEDULER records -- jobs
    12434225 (the failed GTFock run) and 12441235 (the two reference arms that
    converged). Those establish THAT the SCF fails and that the failure is the
    engine's.
  * this file holds the WORKSTATION probes that ask WHY. Sections 1 through 5
    are not SCF runs: each builds J and K once, from one fixed density handed
    identically to both engines, and compares the resulting matrices directly.
    Removing the SCF from the loop is the whole point, because a diverging SCF
    confounds the error in the Fock build with the feedback that error drives,
    and none of those numbers would mean anything if the density kept moving
    underneath them. Section 6 deliberately puts a single iteration back, to
    ask where the divergence starts, and says so where it does.

Every probe ran on one workstation -- cos-4a16505.cos.gatech.edu, an AMD Ryzen
Threadripper 3960X, 24 physical cores and 48 hardware threads -- against the
same build as the Phoenix jobs, through the same GTFockJK path, with
OMPI_MCA_osc=^ucx set. This is not the Phoenix hardware and none of these
numbers are scaling measurements; the quantities compared are matrix elements,
which do not depend on the machine. The thread count differs between probes (48
in section 1, 20 in section 3, 24 in section 6) for scheduling reasons only and
is stated with each result; section 2 varies it deliberately and reports what
that did. The scripts are reproduced verbatim, including the absolute paths
they were run with; those paths are a scratch directory and this worktree, and
are the two things to change to run them elsewhere.

Two cautions apply to every number below.

The comparison density used in sections 1 through 4 is NOT a converged SCF
density -- it cannot be, since the SCF being diagnosed does not converge. It is
the occupied orbitals after a single SCF iteration from a SAD guess, taken from
the run that cached Cocc.npy with maxiter=1 and fail_on_maxiter=False, then
reused verbatim by every later probe. It was produced by GTFock, because at this
size the density-fitting alternative falls back to DiskDFJK and writes ~128 GiB
of scratch.

READ SECTION 5 BEFORE SECTIONS 1 THROUGH 4. That density was finally measured,
late, at max|D| = 1.19e+06, against the ~2 of any physical closed-shell density:
one SCF iteration in this basis had already left the physical range by six
orders of magnitude, and every difference sections 1 through 4 report was
measured there. Their numbers are real and reproducible. The attribution built
on them -- that GTFock's J is 0.31% low -- is not: section 5 repeats the probe
on a physical density of the same molecule in the same basis and finds the two
engines agreeing to eight significant figures. The earlier sections are kept
exactly as they were run, with each withdrawn conclusion marked where it occurs,
because what they actually measured is still evidence -- just evidence about
the density rather than about GTFock. Section 6 then measures that first
iteration itself, through both engines from an identical guess, and finds the
same collapse beginning in the reference run.

The probes report Frobenius norms and element-wise maxima of DIFFERENCES between
two engines on that density. They are meaningful as a comparison between engines
on identical input; they are not error bars on either engine's absolute accuracy,
and no energy should be inferred from them.


1. The basis swap: is it size or is it diffuseness?
---------------------------------------------------

fockdiff3.py builds J and K on protein157 with both DirectJK and GTFockJK, from
one cached density, and reports the difference. It is run twice on the SAME
molecule and geometry with only the basis changed: 6-31G** (1555 functions,
which converges through GTFock) and 6-31+G** (1863, which does not). Holding
everything but the basis fixed is what separates "GTFock degrades with system
size" from "GTFock degrades on diffuse functions" -- the two readings that both
predict a failure in the larger basis alone.

The script also diagonalizes the overlap matrix in the same process, so the
conditioning number quoted next to ||dF|| comes from the same run rather than
from a separate script that might have been given different options.

--- fockdiff3.py, verbatim ---
    """Same measurement as fockdiff2.py, parameterised by basis via FD_BASIS.
    
    fockdiff2 measures the GTFock-vs-DirectJK perturbation on protein157 in
    6-31+G**, the basis whose SCF collapses. On its own that cannot separate two
    explanations:
    
      * the perturbation is ordinary, and 1/s_min amplifies it (conditioning), or
      * the diffuse shells make GTFock's 1e-14 primitive screening genuinely
        worse, so the perturbation is itself larger (screening).
    
    Both would produce a collapse only in the diffuse basis. Running the identical
    probe on 6-31G**, which converges through both engines, is the control that
    tells them apart: comparable ||dF|| means conditioning, a much smaller ||dF||
    means screening is at least partly responsible.
    
    Everything is tagged by basis so the two runs cannot overwrite each other.
    """
    import os, sys, time, numpy as np
    os.environ.setdefault("PSI_SCRATCH", "/fastscratch/awallace43/psi4tmp")
    import psi4
    sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
    from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS
    
    D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
    BASIS = os.environ.get("FD_BASIS", "6-31G**")
    TAG = BASIS.replace("+", "p").replace("*", "s").replace("-", "")
    
    psi4.set_num_threads(48); psi4.set_memory("40 GB")
    psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/fockdiff3_{TAG}.out", False)
    mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
    common = {"basis": BASIS, "puream": False, "guess": "sad",
              "sad_scf_type": "df", "df_scf_guess": False, "ints_tolerance": 1e-12}
    
    cocc_path = f"{D}/Cocc_{TAG}.npy"
    if os.path.exists(cocc_path):
        psi4.set_options({**common, "scf_type": "direct"})
        wfn = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, psi4.core.get_global_option("BASIS"))
        basis = wfn.basisset()
        Cocc = psi4.core.Matrix.from_array(np.load(cocc_path))
        print(f"reused cached Cocc for {BASIS}", flush=True)
    else:
        # NOT "df": MemDFJK wants >100 GiB of AOs at this size and silently falls
        # back to DiskDFJK. Any Cocc works -- both engines are handed the same one.
        psi4.set_options({**common, "scf_type": "gtfock", "maxiter": 1,
                          "fail_on_maxiter": False})
        _, w = psi4.energy("scf", return_wfn=True)
        basis = w.basisset(); Cocc = w.Ca_subset("AO", "OCC")
        np.save(cocc_path, np.asarray(Cocc))
        print(f"built and cached Cocc for {BASIS}", flush=True)
    
    print(f"nbf = {basis.nbf()}   basis = {BASIS}", flush=True)
    
    # The overlap spectrum, so the amplification factor quoted alongside ||dF||
    # comes out of the same process rather than a separate script.
    mints = psi4.core.MintsHelper(basis)
    S = np.asarray(mints.ao_overlap())
    s = np.linalg.eigvalsh(S)
    print(f"s_min = {s.min():.6e}   1/s_min = {1.0/s.min():.3e}", flush=True)
    
    def build(scf_type):
        psi4.set_options({**common, "scf_type": scf_type})
        jk = psi4.core.JK.build_JK(basis, None)
        jk.set_do_J(True); jk.set_do_K(True); jk.initialize()
        jk.C_left_add(Cocc)
        t = time.perf_counter(); jk.compute(); dt = time.perf_counter() - t
        J = np.asarray(jk.J()[0]).copy(); K = np.asarray(jk.K()[0]).copy()
        print(f"[{dt:7.1f}s] {jk.name()} ({scf_type})", flush=True)
        jk.finalize()
        np.save(f"{D}/J_{scf_type}_{TAG}.npy", J); np.save(f"{D}/K_{scf_type}_{TAG}.npy", K)
        return J, K
    
    Jd, Kd = build("direct")
    Jg, Kg = build("gtfock")
    
    def asym(M):
        return np.abs(M - M.T).max()
    
    print()
    print(f"{'quantity':>26} {'direct':>14} {'gtfock':>14}")
    print(f"{'max|J - J^T| (asymmetry)':>26} {asym(Jd):>14.3e} {asym(Jg):>14.3e}")
    print(f"{'max|K - K^T| (asymmetry)':>26} {asym(Kd):>14.3e} {asym(Kg):>14.3e}")
    print(f"{'max|J|':>26} {np.abs(Jd).max():>14.6f} {np.abs(Jg).max():>14.6f}")
    print(f"{'max|K|':>26} {np.abs(Kd).max():>14.6f} {np.abs(Kg).max():>14.6f}")
    print()
    dJ, dK = Jg - Jd, Kg - Kd
    # The SCF diagonalizes X^T F X with F = H + 2J - K, so the Fock perturbation is
    # 2*dJ - dK, not dJ or dK alone. That combination is what 1/s_min multiplies.
    dF = 2.0 * dJ - dK
    print(f"max|dJ| = {np.abs(dJ).max():.6e}   ||dJ||_F = {np.linalg.norm(dJ):.6e}")
    print(f"max|dK| = {np.abs(dK).max():.6e}   ||dK||_F = {np.linalg.norm(dK):.6e}")
    print(f"max|dF| = {np.abs(dF).max():.6e}   ||dF||_F = {np.linalg.norm(dF):.6e}   (dF = 2dJ - dK)")
    print(f"dJ asymmetric part: {asym(dJ):.3e}    dK asymmetric part: {asym(dK):.3e}")
    print(f"amplified ||dF||_F / s_min = {np.linalg.norm(dF)/s.min():.3e}")
    i, j = np.unravel_index(np.abs(dK).argmax(), dK.shape)
    print(f"largest dK at AO ({i},{j}): direct={Kd[i,j]:.8e} gtfock={Kg[i,j]:.8e}")
    print("=== FD3_DONE ===", flush=True)

What the pair says. Adding the diffuse shells to the same molecule multiplies
||dJ||_F by 2872 (2.590981e-03 -> 7.440673e+00) while multiplying ||dK||_F by
only 20.7 (2.947698e-05 -> 6.106648e-04). The two matrices are built by the same
engine in the same call from the same density, so a defect that scaled with
system size, integral count, or contraction depth would have moved them
together. It moved them apart by two orders of magnitude. J is the matrix at
fault, and 1555 -> 1863 functions is far too small a step to explain a factor of
2872 by size.

The element-wise view says the same thing more bluntly. In the diffuse basis
GTFock's largest J element comes back 39.382141 against DirectJK's 39.535276 --
0.387% low -- while its largest K element is 4.916480 against 4.916480, equal in
every digit printed. Both engines return exactly symmetric J and K (the
asymmetry rows are 0.000e+00 throughout), so this is not a transposition or
accumulation-order artifact.

[WITHDRAWN, see section 5. The 6-31+G** density this is measured on has max|D| =
2.38e+06 while the 6-31G** density it is compared against has max|D| = 2.08. The
comparison changes the density and the basis at the same time. On matched
physical densities the two bases agree, and J and K behave alike.]

Conditioning is measured here, not assumed, and it is a multiplier rather than a
cause. The 1863-function overlap matrix has s_min = 1.089528e-07, only 9% above
the S_TOLERANCE default of 1e-7 (psi4/src/read_options.cc), so Psi4 orthogonalizes
symmetrically instead of canonically and the Fock perturbation is amplified by
about 9.2e+06; the 1555-function basis sits at 3.321363e-04, three thousand-fold
better conditioned. The amplified quantity differs by 8.8e+06 between the two
legs while ||dF||_F itself differs by only 2.9e+03, so conditioning is doing most
of the arithmetic work in the collapse. But it cannot be the cause: DirectJK and
density fitting cross that same near-singular basis and converge in 12 monotone
iterations (job 12441235), and they do so because their own dF is zero. An
amplifier with nothing to amplify is harmless. This is why the doc bullet says
near-singularity "compounds the failure without causing it", and why an earlier
draft that blamed conditioning was withdrawn rather than published.


2. The rank and thread sweep: is any of this caused by distribution?
---------------------------------------------------------------------

A 0.31% uniform shortfall in J with a 19% non-uniform residual has the shape of
a small fraction of work units going missing. If that were what it is, the
fraction would move when the work is partitioned differently. If it is instead a
deterministic screening or extent decision, it is a property of the basis and
will not move at all. Those predictions are opposite, so one sweep settles it.

This also extends acceptance criterion 1 to the place it matters most: rank-count
invariance is proven by the test suite at small sizes, and this asks whether it
survives at 1863 functions in the basis where the engine actually fails.

epsranks.py rebuilds J with GTFockJK from the same cached density used above and
compares it to the cached J_direct, reporting the best-fit uniform deficit
eps = -<Jd,dJ>/<Jd,Jd>, the residual fraction left after removing that uniform
part, and the trace ratio.

--- epsranks.py, verbatim ---
    """Does the 0.31% J deficit depend on rank count or thread count?
    
    The deficit is a uniform -0.31% scaling of J with 19% residual noise, which is
    the shape of a small random fraction of WORK UNITS going missing rather than an
    integral-accuracy limit. If that is what it is, the fraction should move with
    how the work is partitioned. If it is instead a deterministic screening or
    extent decision, the fraction is a property of the basis and will not move at
    all. Those two predictions are opposite, so one sweep separates them.
    
    This also bears directly on acceptance criterion 1: rank-count invariance is
    proven at small sizes, and this asks whether it still holds at 1863 basis
    functions in a diffuse basis -- the point where the engine actually fails.
    
    Compares against the cached J_direct for the same cached density, so every point
    in the sweep is the same computation with only the partitioning changed.
    """
    import os, sys, time, numpy as np
    os.environ.setdefault("PSI_SCRATCH", "/fastscratch/awallace43/psi4tmp")
    import psi4
    sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
    from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS
    
    D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
    RANK = int(os.environ.get("OMPI_COMM_WORLD_RANK", "0"))
    NRANK = int(os.environ.get("OMPI_COMM_WORLD_SIZE", "1"))
    NTHREAD = int(os.environ.get("ER_THREADS", "24"))
    
    psi4.set_num_threads(NTHREAD)
    psi4.set_memory("30 GB")
    psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/eps_ranks_r{RANK}of{NRANK}_t{NTHREAD}.out", False)
    
    mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
    psi4.set_options({"basis": "6-31+G**", "puream": False,
                      "ints_tolerance": 1e-12, "scf_type": "gtfock"})
    wfn = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, psi4.core.get_global_option("BASIS"))
    basis = wfn.basisset()
    Cocc = psi4.core.Matrix.from_array(np.load(f"{D}/Cocc_631pGss.npy"))
    
    jk = psi4.core.JK.build_JK(basis, None)
    jk.set_do_J(True); jk.set_do_K(False); jk.initialize()
    jk.C_left_add(Cocc)
    t = time.perf_counter(); jk.compute(); dt = time.perf_counter() - t
    Jg = np.asarray(jk.J()[0]).copy()
    name = jk.name()
    jk.finalize()
    
    if RANK == 0:
        Jd = np.load(f"{D}/J_direct_631pGss.npy")
        dJ = Jg - Jd
        x, y = Jd.ravel(), dJ.ravel()
        eps = -(x @ y) / (x @ x)
        resid = np.linalg.norm(dJ + eps * Jd) / max(np.linalg.norm(dJ), 1e-300)
        print(f"ERANK ranks={NRANK} threads={NTHREAD} {name:>10s} "
              f"eps={eps:.6e} maxdJ={np.abs(dJ).max():.6e} "
              f"normdJ={np.linalg.norm(dJ):.6e} resid_frac={resid:.3f} "
              f"trace_ratio={np.trace(Jg)/np.trace(Jd):.8f} t={dt:.1f}s", flush=True)

Every reported digit is identical across 1, 2 and 4 ranks: the same best-fit
deficit, the same largest element, the same Frobenius norm, the same residual
fraction, the same trace ratio. GTFock partitions the work genuinely differently
at each point -- its own log shows the Fock-block dimensions changing from
(1863 1863 1863 1863) at one rank to (1863 940 1863 1863) at two and
(940 940 1863 1863) at four -- and the answer does not move.

That closes the distribution question in the negative. maxdJ and normdJ here also
reproduce the single-process fockdiff3.py numbers above exactly, which is a
second, independent check: two scripts, two invocation paths, one answer.

The wall time is the only thing that moves across the three rank rows, and it
moves the right way (1544.7 -> 714.4 -> 473.9 s, 2.16x and 3.26x), so the ranks
really were doing the divided work rather than each quietly repeating it.

The last three rows vary the other axis. Holding the rank count at one and
sweeping 1, 4, 8 and 24 threads changes the shared-memory partitioning
completely: GTFock sizes its per-thread Fock blocking buffers from the thread
count and hands out shell quartets over threads, and at one thread that whole
layer is gone. Every reported digit is identical at all four points. The wall
time spans 4957.3 -> 1544.7 -> 881.3 -> 405.4 s, a 12.2x speedup over a 24x
thread range, so the threads were dividing real work rather than repeating it
and the invariance is not the trivial kind. Combined with the rank sweep, the
deficit is invariant under both levels of parallelism GTFock has and not only
the distributed one, which is what makes "deterministic property of the
integral path" the reading that survives rather than merely the one left over.

What survives, then, is a deterministic property of the integral path itself in
a diffuse basis. eps = 3.101426e-03 is a uniform 0.31% shortfall; resid_frac =
0.188 says 19% of the difference is not uniform, so it is not a single global
scale factor either. Both parts are reproducible to every digit.


3. The screening control: is the deficit real, or is the probe density?
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sections 1 and 2 establish that the difference is in J, follows the diffuse
shells, and does not depend on the rank count. [The first two of those are
WITHDRAWN, see section 5; the rank-count result stands. The section is preserved
because the question it asks next is the right one, and because the answer it
gives to that question is wrong in an instructive way.] None of that yet rules out the
least interesting explanation: that this density is so badly conditioned as a
probe that NO integral engine could be trusted on it, and the 0.31% is measuring
the probe rather than GTFock. The density's J is a difference of much larger
cancelling contributions, so this is a real possibility and not a formality.

screentest.py answers it by measuring the spread among Psi4's OWN J builds on the
identical density, with the screening deliberately varied over a range far wider
than any defensible setting: two tolerances four orders of magnitude apart, three
different screening algorithms including the density-aware one, and one run with
screening switched off entirely. That spread is the resolution of the whole
experiment. Nothing smaller than it can be attributed to any engine.

--- screentest.py, verbatim ---
    """Is the 0.31% J deficit a GTFock defect, or an artifact of the probe density?
    
    The probe density at protein157/6-31+G** has elements of order 1e6 cancelling to
    tr(DS)=580 exactly, so J[D] is a difference of large numbers. Two very different
    readings survive that:
    
      (a) GTFock drops contributions a density-aware builder keeps, and the deficit
          is real. Then Psi4's OWN builder should move when its screening is changed,
          and the density-aware setting should sit apart from the blind ones.
      (b) No engine can be trusted on a density this pathological, and the deficit
          measures the probe rather than GTFock. Then Psi4's builders will scatter
          among THEMSELVES by a comparable amount, and the GTFock gap is not special.
    
    Psi4's default CSAM screening is itself density-blind and at 1e-12 is LOOSER than
    GTFock's hardcoded 1e-14, so the naive "GTFock screens too aggressively" story
    already fails on its face; this measures what is actually happening instead. The
    spread among the Psi4 variants is the control: it is the resolution below which
    nothing can be concluded about GTFock.
    """
    import os, sys, time, numpy as np
    os.environ.setdefault("PSI_SCRATCH", "/fastscratch/awallace43/psi4tmp")
    import psi4
    sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
    from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS
    D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
    psi4.set_num_threads(int(os.environ.get("ST_THREADS", "24"))); psi4.set_memory("40 GB")
    psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/screentest.out", False)
    
    mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
    base = {"basis": "6-31+G**", "puream": False}
    psi4.set_options({**base, "scf_type": "direct"})
    wfn = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, psi4.core.get_global_option("BASIS"))
    basis = wfn.basisset()

4. The orthogonalization remedy: is the near-singular overlap the cause?
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 1 measured a minimum overlap eigenvalue of 1.089528e-07 for the failing
basis, 9% above Psi4's default S_TOLERANCE of 1e-7. Psi4 therefore keeps every
function and orthogonalizes symmetrically, and symmetric orthogonalization
amplifies an error in the Fock build by 1/s_min -- about 9.2e+06 here. That is
a natural suspect and worth testing rather than arguing about, so PACE job
12441826 tested it: one 24-core node, the same geometry, basis, guess and
convergence thresholds as the benchmark, maxiter 60, and S_TOLERANCE raised to
1e-6 as the only departure. Raising the threshold above s_min is what makes
Psi4 switch to canonical orthogonalization and project the offending directions
out entirely.

Before reading the result it is worth knowing exactly how much that threshold
change removes, which is a property of the overlap matrix alone and costs no
integrals to measure.

5. The physical-density control: was any of it GTFock's?
--------------------------------------------------------

Sections 1 through 4 all measure the same thing: a difference between two
engines handed one cached density. That design is sound only if the density is
one the engines could actually be asked for in a converged calculation. Nothing
above checked that. Section 3's title asks whether the deficit is real or the
probe density is, but what section 3 varied was Psi4's screening on the SAME
density; it never varied the density. The control below does, and it is the one
that decides the question.

The check that should have come first costs no integrals. A closed-shell density
normalised so that tr(DS) is the electron count has elements of order 1, and at
most 2 -- two electrons in one orbital on one function. Measuring the three
cached densities:

    density                              max|D|        ||D||_F
    6-31G**   after one SCF iteration   2.0770e+00   2.4338e+01
    6-31+G**  after one SCF iteration   2.3847e+06   9.6701e+06
    6-31+G**  converged 6-31G** proj.   2.0770e+00   2.4338e+01

The middle row is the density every number in sections 1 through 4 was measured
on. One SCF iteration in the diffuse basis had already carried it six orders of
magnitude out of physical range. It is not a guess density that happens to be
unconverged; it is wreckage produced by the very failure being diagnosed. The
first and third rows agree to five figures because the projection is Lowdin-
symmetrised and preserves the metric, which is also the check that the
projection did not invent anything.

So the probe density had to be replaced with one that is delocalized -- unlike
the SAD guess, which is block diagonal by atom and so blind to any inter-atomic
effect -- and physical, unlike the iterate. The converged 6-31G** density
projected into 6-31+G** is both, and it is a density this molecule genuinely
has.

--- physdens.py, verbatim ---

"""Compare the engines at protein157/6-31+G** on a PHYSICAL delocalized density.

Two densities have already been compared at this point and they disagree about
whether GTFock is broken:

  SAD (block diagonal by atom, physical)   -> engines agree to 1.2e-06 Eh
  SCF iterate in this near-singular basis  -> GTFock's J is 0.31% low

Those differ in two ways at once, delocalization and scale, so neither explains
the other. This supplies the missing third density: delocalized like the second,
physical like the first. It is the converged 6-31G** density -- measured max|D| =
2.08 -- projected into 6-31+G**, so the comparison changes only the pathology and
holds the delocalization fixed.

If the engines agree here, the 0.31% belongs to the probe density and not to
GTFock, and the SCF failure is about what the near-singular basis does to the
iterates. If they still disagree, it is GTFock's, on a density no one can call
unreasonable. The projection is validated (orthonormality, electron count,
density scale) before any of it is believed.
"""
import os, sys, time, numpy as np
os.environ.setdefault("PSI_SCRATCH", "/fastscratch/awallace43/psi4tmp")
import psi4
sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS
D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
psi4.set_num_threads(int(os.environ.get("PD_THREADS", "24"))); psi4.set_memory("40 GB")
psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/physdens.out", False)

mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
def build_basis(name):
    psi4.set_options({"basis": name, "puream": False, "scf_type": "direct"})
    w = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, psi4.core.get_global_option("BASIS"))
    return w.basisset(), w.nalpha()

bs_old, ndocc = build_basis("6-31G**")
bs_new, ndocc_new = build_basis("6-31+G**")
assert ndocc == ndocc_new, (ndocc, ndocc_new)
C_old = np.load(f"{D}/Cocc_631Gss.npy")
assert C_old.shape == (bs_old.nbf(), ndocc), (C_old.shape, bs_old.nbf(), ndocc)

mints = psi4.core.MintsHelper(bs_new)
S_nn = np.asarray(mints.ao_overlap())
S_no = np.asarray(mints.ao_overlap(bs_new, bs_old))
assert S_no.shape == (bs_new.nbf(), bs_old.nbf()), S_no.shape

# Least-squares projection. S_nn is near singular (s_min ~ 1e-7), so a plain
# inverse would inject exactly the near-null-space blow-up this probe exists to
# avoid; the pseudoinverse cutoff discards it instead.
C_proj = np.linalg.pinv(S_nn, rcond=1e-6) @ (S_no @ C_old)
M = C_proj.T @ S_nn @ C_proj
w, V = np.linalg.eigh(M)
print(f"projection metric eigenvalues: min={w.min():.6e} max={w.max():.6e}", flush=True)
Cocc = C_proj @ (V @ np.diag(w ** -0.5) @ V.T)

orth = np.abs(Cocc.T @ S_nn @ Cocc - np.eye(ndocc)).max()

print(f"VALIDATE  max|C^T S C - I| = {orth:.3e}   tr(DS) = {nelec:.6f} "
      f"(expect {2*ndocc})   max|D| = {np.abs(Dm).max():.4e}   "
      f"||D||_F = {np.linalg.norm(Dm):.4e}", flush=True)
if orth > 1e-8 or abs(nelec - 2 * ndocc) > 1e-6:
    print("PROJECTION REJECTED: not an orthonormal N-electron density; "
          "nothing below this line would mean anything.", flush=True)
    sys.exit(1)
np.save(f"{D}/Cocc_proj_631pGss.npy", Cocc)
Cm = psi4.core.Matrix.from_array(np.ascontiguousarray(Cocc))

def buildJK(scf_type):
    psi4.set_options({"basis": "6-31+G**", "puream": False,
                      "ints_tolerance": 1e-12, "scf_type": scf_type})
    jk = psi4.core.JK.build_JK(bs_new, None)
    jk.set_do_J(True); jk.set_do_K(True); jk.initialize()
    jk.C_left_add(Cm)
    t = time.perf_counter(); jk.compute(); dt = time.perf_counter() - t
    J = np.asarray(jk.J()[0]).copy(); K = np.asarray(jk.K()[0]).copy()
    nm = jk.name(); jk.finalize()
    print(f"  [{dt:7.1f}s] {nm} ({scf_type})", flush=True)
    return J, K

Jd, Kd = buildJK("direct")
Jg, Kg = buildJK("gtfock")
dJ, dK = Jg - Jd, Kg - Kd
x = Jd.ravel()
eps = -(x @ dJ.ravel()) / (x @ x)
resid = np.linalg.norm(dJ + eps * Jd) / max(np.linalg.norm(dJ), 1e-300)
dF = 2.0 * dJ - dK
e2 = 2.0 * np.sum(Dm * dJ) - np.sum(Dm * dK)
print(f"\nPHYS eps={eps:.6e} resid_frac={resid:.3f} "
      f"maxdJ={np.abs(dJ).max():.6e} normdJ={np.linalg.norm(dJ):.6e} "
      f"maxdK={np.abs(dK).max():.6e} normdK={np.linalg.norm(dK):.6e} "
      f"normdF={np.linalg.norm(dF):.6e} dE2={e2:.6e} Eh "
      f"trace_ratio={np.trace(Jg)/np.trace(Jd):.8f}", flush=True)

What falls
~~~~~~~~~~

Every claim of the form "GTFock's J is 0.31% low" is withdrawn. GTFock's J is
0.31% low on one specific wrecked density, and so, for all this file shows, is
any J built by any method that screens at all. Specifically:

  - Section 1's conclusion that the fault "follows the diffuse shells rather
    than the size" is confounded: the 6-31G** arm was measured on a physical
    density (max|D| = 2.08) and the 6-31+G** arm on a wrecked one
    (max|D| = 2.38e+06). Two things changed between the arms, not one. On
    matched physical densities the two bases give the same answer.
  - Section 1's contrast between J and K -- J 0.31% low, K within 1e-06 -- is
    measured on that same density. It remains true OF that density and is no
    longer evidence that J is where GTFock breaks.
  - Section 3's conclusion that GTFock's difference is a different kind from
    any Psi4 screening choice is withdrawn. On a physical density it has the
    same resid_frac = 1.000 scattered signature they do.
  - Section 4's energy arithmetic -- that -eps * E_J = -51.6 Eh accounts for
    the failing run's -52.7 Eh offset -- is withdrawn. Both quantities are
    properties of diverged states, and matching two numbers that each come
    from the wreckage does not establish that one causes the other.
  - The primitive-screen hypothesis, which the doc bullet used to advance and
    no longer does, loses its supporting evidence. It is not disproven --
    GTFock does screen primitives at a hardcoded 1e-14 that INTS_TOLERANCE
    cannot reach, and that is still a real property of the stack, measurable
    on the six-water cluster -- but nothing in this file now argues that it is
    what breaks this SCF.

What stands
~~~~~~~~~~~

The user-facing restriction is unaffected, because it never depended on the
mechanism. GTFock runs the full 100 iterations on protein157/6-31+G** (job
12434225) while DirectJK converges it to -3642.752751 and DiskDFJK to
-3642.748403, each in 12 monotone iterations from the same build (job 12441235).
Diffuse functions on a system this size remain unsupported.

Also unaffected:

  - Rank and thread invariance, section 2. Every digit is identical at 1, 2 and
    4 ranks and at 1, 4, 8 and 24 threads, with a 12.2x wall-clock span across
    the thread sweep. Whatever this is, it is not a distribution bug. This was
    always the strongest result in the file and it needed no density
    assumption: it compares GTFock to GTFock.
  - The measured cost of Psi4's own screening controls, section 3: at most
    5.19e-04 in Frobenius norm against an unscreened build. That is a
    measurement of Psi4, not of GTFock, and it stands.
  - The overlap spectra, section 4: s_min = 1.089528e-07 for 6-31+G** with 8
    eigenvalues below 1e-6 and 22 below 1e-5, against s_min = 3.321363e-04 for
    6-31G** with nothing below 1e-5.
  - Job 12441826: S_TOLERANCE raised to 1e-6 does not rescue the SCF.

What this reopens
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Section 4 closed with "conditioning is now excluded twice". That is wrong in
both halves and is retracted here.

It was excluded as a CAUSE on the grounds that DirectJK and density fitting
cross the same near-singular overlap in 12 iterations. That does not follow.
Symmetric orthogonalization amplifies a PERTURBATION to the Fock build by
1/s_min, about 9.2e+06 here. DirectJK has no perturbation with respect to
itself, so there is nothing for its 9.2e+06 to act on, and its converging is
exactly what a conditioning-amplification mechanism predicts rather than
evidence against one. What the reference runs establish is that the basis alone
is survivable, not that the basis is innocent when combined with a second
engine.

It was excluded as a REMEDY on the grounds that S_TOLERANCE 1e-6 left the
failure intact. But section 4 measured, and then did not use, how weak that
remedy is: raising the threshold to 1e-6 discards 8 directions out of 1863 and
moves s_min from 1.089528e-07 to the ninth eigenvalue at 1.050e-06. The
amplification goes from 9.18e+06 to 9.52e+05. One decade. A remedy that buys one
decade failing does not exclude a mechanism that may need four.

The consistent reading of everything now measured is that a small engine
difference -- ||dF||_F = 1.47e-02 on a physical density, which is ordinary
screening-level disagreement -- meets an amplification of 9.2e+06 in this basis
and destroys the iterate, after which the engines are being asked about a
density that no longer resembles one. On that reading the 0.31% is the
consequence and the conditioning is the cause. This is a hypothesis, stated as
one. It predicts that the blow-up is visible at the very first iteration, which
is testable and cheap. Section 6 tests it, and it survives.

One thing that reading must not be allowed to say -- and that an earlier draft
of this paragraph did say -- is that the engines diverge on the wrecked iterate
because they are "being asked about a matrix with elements of 1e+06". Size
alone cannot do it. J is linear in D, so under D -> cD both J and dJ scale by
c, and

    eps = -<Jref,dJ>/<Jref,Jref>      resid_frac = ||dJ + eps*Jref||/||dJ||

are left exactly where they were. A density scaled up by a million gives the
same eps as the physical density it was scaled from. What separates eps =
3.1e-03 from eps = 3.6e-09 therefore cannot be the magnitude of the probe and
can only be its DIRECTION. Section 6 measures both halves of that: it confirms
the scale invariance on the engines themselves rather than assuming it, and it
shows where in the basis the wrecked iterate points.


6. Where it is born: the first iteration
----------------------------------------

Section 5 ended with a hypothesis and a cheap prediction: that an ordinary
screening-level engine difference meets this basis's 9.2e+06 amplification and
destroys the iterate, and that if that is what happens, the damage should be
visible at the very first iteration. This section runs that test and the two
controls that go with it. Everything below is protein157 in 6-31+G**,
Cartesian, one rank, 24 threads, on the same build as sections 1 through 5.

One iteration from an identical guess
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

iter1.py hands each engine the same SAD guess and the same options and stops
after exactly one iteration, so the only thing that differs between the two
arms is the J/K build inside that single iteration.

--- iter1.py, verbatim ---

Where the iterates sit in the overlap spectrum
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Section 5 argued that the difference had to be one of direction rather than
magnitude, but it never measured a direction. This does, and it costs no
integrals at all: every occupied-coefficient matrix involved is already on disk
from the probes above, and the whole measurement is one 1863x1863 symmetric
eigendecomposition.

--- nullweight.py, verbatim ---
"""Does the wrecked iterate live in the near-null subspace of the overlap?

Section 5 argued from linearity that the size of a density cannot change eps,
so if the wrecked iterate disagrees where a physical one agrees, the difference
has to be in DIRECTION. This measures the direction directly, and needs no
integrals: every occupied-coefficient matrix involved is already on disk.

Write S = U diag(s) U^T and y = U^T C, so w_k = ||y_k||^2 is the weight the
occupied space puts on overlap eigenvector k. Two identities pin the picture:

    sum_k s_k w_k = tr(C^T S C) = nocc     (C is S-orthonormal, always)
    sum_k w_k     = ||C||_F^2              (unconstrained, can be anything)

The first holds for every iterate no matter how wrecked, because every iterate
is a projector onto 290 occupied orbitals -- iter1.py measured tr(DS) = 580.000
for both engines. So a density can only acquire large elements by moving its
weight onto SMALL s_k. The ratio

    s_eff = nocc / ||C||_F^2

is the s-weighted mean eigenvalue the occupied space sits on: O(1) for a
physical density, and driven toward s_min for one that has fallen into the
near-null directions. That single number is the direction, quantified.
"""
import numpy as np, psi4, sys
sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS

D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/nullweight.out", False)
mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
psi4.set_options({"basis": "6-31+G**", "puream": False})
bs = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, "6-31+G**").basisset()
S = np.asarray(psi4.core.MintsHelper(bs).ao_overlap())
s, U = np.linalg.eigh(S)
print(f"nbf={len(s)}  s_min={s.min():.6e}  below 1e-6: {(s<1e-6).sum()}  "
      f"below 1e-5: {(s<1e-5).sum()}  below 1e-4: {(s<1e-4).sum()}", flush=True)

cases = [("physical (proj. from 6-31G**)", "Cocc_proj_631pGss.npy"),
         ("iter 1, DirectJK",              "Cocc_iter1_direct.npy"),
         ("iter 1, GTFockJK",              "Cocc_iter1_gtfock.npy"),
         ("iter 1, GTFockJK (cached)",     "Cocc_631pGss.npy")]

print(f"\n{'density':32s} {'||C||_F^2':>12s} {'s_eff':>11s} "
      f"{'w(s<1e-6)':>10s} {'w(s<1e-5)':>10s} {'w(s<1e-4)':>10s}")
for label, fn in cases:
    C = np.load(f"{D}/{fn}")
    w = np.einsum("kj,kj->k", U.T @ C, U.T @ C)
    n2, nocc = w.sum(), C.shape[1]
    chk = float(s @ w)
    f = [w[s < t].sum() / n2 for t in (1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4)]
    print(f"{label:32s} {n2:12.4e} {nocc/n2:11.4e} "
          f"{f[0]:10.6f} {f[1]:10.6f} {f[2]:10.6f}   [sum s*w = {chk:.4f}, nocc = {nocc}]")

That is the strongest support the conditioning hypothesis has, and it is also
the honest limit of it. The mechanism section 5 proposed is a linear-response
argument: a perturbation ||dF||_F = 1.47e-02 amplified by 1/s_min = 9.2e+06.
That estimate gives 1.3e+05, comfortably more than the 2.9e+03 growth in
||C||_F actually seen between the two first iterates, so the mechanism has
enough headroom to produce what is observed. But an estimate that overshoots by
a factor of 46 establishes only that the effect is available, not that it is
the one acting; and the reference iterate is not a small perturbation of the
physical density either, so linear response is being invoked where it does not
strictly hold. 1/s_min is a scale here, not a calculation.

Scale or direction?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The linearity argument in section 5 is a statement about the engines, not a
theorem about the code: an engine that screened on the density would break it,
and screening is the whole subject of this file. scaletest.py measures it
instead of asserting it, by rebuilding J and K from both engines on the
physical projected density scaled by 1e+06 -- putting its elements in the same
range as the wrecked iterate while leaving its direction untouched.

--- scaletest.py, verbatim ---
"""Is the disagreement about the density's SIZE or its DIRECTION?

Section 5 replaced a probe density with max|D| = 2.4e+06 by a physical one with
max|D| = 2.08 and the engines went from disagreeing at 3.1e-03 to agreeing at
3.6e-09. The obvious reading -- that the huge elements are what break the
comparison -- is wrong on its face, because J is LINEAR in D. Under D -> cD both
J and dJ scale by c, so

    eps = -<J,dJ>/<J,J>    and    resid_frac = ||dJ + eps J|| / ||dJ||

are both invariant. Magnitude cannot move them. What can move them is DIRECTION:
which subspace the density occupies. The wrecked iterate is dominated by the
near-null directions of S, where diffuse functions almost coincide and screening
decisions are most delicate; the physical density is not.

Linearity is an assumption about the engines, though, not a theorem about the
code: an engine that screens on the density would violate it. GTFock screens
Schwarz-style on primitive pairs at a fixed 1e-14 with no reference to D, so it
should be exactly linear. This measures that instead of asserting it.

The physical density from physdens.py is rebuilt scaled by 1e+06 -- putting its
elements in the same range as the wrecked iterate while leaving its direction
untouched. If eps stays at 3.6e-09, magnitude is irrelevant and the 0.31%
belongs to the wrecked density's direction. If eps moves toward 3.1e-03, an
engine is screening on the density and the story is different.
"""
import os, sys, time, numpy as np
os.environ.setdefault("PSI_SCRATCH", "/fastscratch/awallace43/psi4tmp")
import psi4
sys.path.insert(0, "/home/awallace43/.treehouse/psi4-1ae95c/2/psi4/tests/pytests")
from gtfock_hpc_benchmark import _SYSTEMS

D = "/tmp/fm-psi4-djk/diag"
SCALE = 1.0e6
psi4.set_num_threads(24); psi4.set_memory("40 GB")
psi4.core.set_output_file(f"{D}/scaletest.out", False)
mol = psi4.geometry(_SYSTEMS["protein157"])
common = {"basis": "6-31+G**", "puream": False, "guess": "sad",
          "sad_scf_type": "df", "df_scf_guess": False, "ints_tolerance": 1e-12}
psi4.set_options({**common, "scf_type": "direct"})
basis = psi4.core.Wavefunction.build(mol, "6-31+G**").basisset()

C = np.load(f"{D}/Cocc_proj_631pGss.npy") * np.sqrt(SCALE)
Dm = 2.0 * C @ C.T
print(f"scaled physical density: max|D|={np.abs(Dm).max():.4e} "
      f"||D||_F={np.linalg.norm(Dm):.4e}  (scale={SCALE:g})", flush=True)
Cm = psi4.core.Matrix.from_array(np.ascontiguousarray(C))

out = {}
for scf_type in ("direct", "gtfock"):
    t = time.time()
    psi4.set_options({**common, "scf_type": scf_type})
    jk = psi4.core.JK.build_JK(basis, None)
    jk.set_do_J(True); jk.set_do_K(True); jk.initialize()
    jk.C_left_add(Cm)          # symmetric build: C_right_add would make GTFock
    jk.compute()               # refuse it, and is not what physdens.py did
    out[scf_type] = (np.array(jk.J()[0]), np.array(jk.K()[0]))
    np.save(f"{D}/J_{scf_type}_scaled.npy", out[scf_type][0])
    np.save(f"{D}/K_{scf_type}_scaled.npy", out[scf_type][1])
    jk.finalize()
    print(f"  [{time.time()-t:7.1f}s] {scf_type}", flush=True)

Jd, Kd = out["direct"]; Jg, Kg = out["gtfock"]
dJ, dK = Jg - Jd, Kg - Kd
eps = -np.einsum("ij,ij->", Jd, dJ) / np.einsum("ij,ij->", Jd, Jd)
resid = np.linalg.norm(dJ + eps * Jd) / np.linalg.norm(dJ)
print(f"SCALED eps={eps:.6e} resid_frac={resid:.3f} maxdJ={np.abs(dJ).max():.6e} "
      f"normdJ={np.linalg.norm(dJ):.6e} normdK={np.linalg.norm(dK):.6e}", flush=True)
print(f"  unscaled reference from physdens: eps=3.611712e-09 resid_frac=1.000 "
      f"normdJ=7.341583e-03", flush=True)
print(f"  linearity check: normdJ/scale = {np.linalg.norm(dJ)/SCALE:.6e} "
      f"(expect 7.341583e-03)", flush=True)

eps comes back at 3.611711e-09 against the unscaled 3.611712e-09, agreeing to

scaled by the full factor of a million to 7.341583e+03, divides back down to

engines are exactly linear in the density across six orders of magnitude of it.

So magnitude is not merely irrelevant in theory, it is measurably irrelevant
here. A density with max|D| = 2.08e+06 and the physical density it was scaled
from give the same eps to the digits printed. Whatever made the wrecked iterate
disagree at eps = 3.1e-03 was its direction, which the previous subsection
located: 99.87% of it inside the subspace where the overlap eigenvalue is below
1e-4.

What section 6 establishes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - The divergence is created in the first iteration. From an identical SAD
    guess, one iteration separates the engines by 59.1 Eh and their iterates'
    max|D| by a factor of 8.4e+03. None of the failure requires 100 iterations
    to appear.
  - The first iterate of BOTH engines collapses into the near-null subspace of
    the overlap. That is a property of protein157 in 6-31+G** and not of
    GTFock: DirectJK puts 71.6% of its occupied weight on the 8 overlap
    eigenvectors below 1e-6 and still converges in 12 iterations.
  - GTFock's iterate goes a further 6.3e+03 down, to s_eff = 3.3e-05, which is
    only 302 times s_min, and does not come back.
  - Every iterate here is a valid S-orthonormal projector onto 290 occupied
    orbitals, tr(DS) = 580.000000 exactly. The failure is in which subspace the
    orbitals occupy, not in the algebraic form of the density.
  - Both engines are linear in the density over six orders of magnitude, so
    the size of a probe density cannot affect eps and never could have. Only
    its direction can.
  - The cached probe density of sections 1 through 3 is reproducible: a fresh
    GTFock first iterate lands within 0.48% of it.

What is still open
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The cause. Everything above is consistent with section 5's hypothesis and none
of it proves that hypothesis. The specific gap is that nothing here connects
the engine difference measured on a physical density, ||dF||_F = 1.47e-02, to
the depth GTFock's iterate actually reaches, other than by an
order-of-magnitude estimate that overshoots by a factor of 46. Closing that
would need the perturbation measured on the guess density itself and the
eigenvalue gaps of the guess Fock matrix, neither of which is in this file.

Two cheaper experiments would sharpen it and are not done:

  - Whether DirectJK, restarted from GTFock's first iterate, recovers. If it
    does, GTFock's damage is a one-time push into a bad region rather than a
    difference the engine keeps re-injecting; if it does not, the region is
    simply unreachable by this SCF and the engine choice only decides who falls
    into it. Cocc_iter1_gtfock.npy is on disk for exactly this.
  - Whether canonical orthogonalization, which drops near-null directions
    outright rather than raising a threshold inside symmetric
    orthogonalization, changes the outcome. Section 4's S_TOLERANCE probe moved
    s_min by one decade and is not this test.

Until one of those is run the user-facing statement stays where section 5 left
it: the restriction is measured, distribution is excluded, GTFock's accuracy on
physical densities in this basis is established, and the cause is open with the
conditioning of the basis as the leading suspect.
